1. Executive Summary
Igbo Wikimedians User Group (also referred to as Igbo Wikimedians, Wikimedia Igbo, or IWUG) is a volunteer community working to improve the availability, quality, and visibility of Igbo-language and Igbo-related knowledge across Wikipedia and other Wikimedia projects. Its documented work spans encyclopedia articles, dictionary and lexeme data, source texts, structured data, photographs, audio recordings, cultural heritage, contributor training, and community development.
The group's official history identifies the Wikipedia Igbo Women Edit-a-thon Project in 2016 as the beginning of its organized activities. The Wikimedia Affiliations Committee approved recognition of the group as a Wikimedia user group on 5 May 2018. Subsequent public records describe expansion into Wikidata lexemes, Wikisource, Commons media, technical maintenance, university clubs, and recurring virtual campaigns.
A Wikimedia Community Fund General Support Fund grant record covers 1 January 2024 through 31 December 2026 and lists a recommended amount of US$243,000. The grant amount should not be interpreted as proof that the full sum was paid or spent. The public financial record also contains a 2024 category-total discrepancy that should be checked against the original financial attachment or a corrected statement.
This dossier is a substantial first-pass compilation of public primary sources and reported results. It is not an assertion that every social-media post, historical page, grant attachment, contributor record, or private discussion has been captured or independently audited.
2. Identity, Mission, and Organizational Model
The group is a Wikimedia movement affiliate organized around an Igbo-language and cultural community. Its public objectives include encouraging Igbo speakers and learners to contribute to Wikimedia projects, improving content coverage and quality, developing contributors' skills, supporting language and cultural preservation, and working with institutions and other organizations to promote free knowledge.
It is important to distinguish Wikimedia from Wikipedia. Wikipedia is the encyclopedia; Wikimedia is the broader movement and project ecosystem, including Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons, Wikidata, Wiktionary, Wikisource, and Wikiquote. The user group organizes people and projects around Igbo language and culture across that wider ecosystem.
The group's activities are therefore not limited to writing encyclopedia articles. They include language data, pronunciation recordings, structured knowledge, digitized texts, images, technical cleanup, and the development of contributors and local communities.

3. Founding History and Recognition
The official profile identifies the Wikipedia Igbo Women Edit-a-thon Project in 2016 as the beginning of organized activity. The project aimed to improve representation of notable Igbo women on Igbo Wikipedia through the creation and translation of articles.
The Wikimedia Affiliations Committee approved recognition of the group as a Wikimedia user group on 5 May 2018. The official profile lists the following people as founding members: Ozurumba Uzoma, Tochi Precious, Blossom Ozurumba, and Oby Ezeilo. The profile also identifies Tochi Precious and Blossom Ozurumba as designated Wikimedia Foundation contacts in that historical listing. These are historical records and do not confirm current roles.
Development milestones
•	2016 — The Wikipedia Igbo Women Edit-a-thon Project marks the beginning of organized activities described in the group's official history.
•	5 May 2018 — Recognition as a Wikimedia user group is approved.
•	2021 — The group reports selection of Igbo as a focus language for Wikidata lexicographical data and held its first Wikidata Igbo Lexemes Training on 10–11 July.
•	2023 — Public reports show expanded activity in university outreach, translation, monuments, structured data, and Wikisource.
•	2024–2026 — A multi-year General Support Fund grant supports a wider program of community and content activities.
•	2025–2026 — Public activity reports increasingly emphasize maintenance, language data quality, audio documentation, and recurring technical training.

A 2026 project page describes a project launched online on 4 April, followed by a street and market interview challenge in Awka on 10 April. A separate project summary describes 11 registered contributors documenting 55 proverbs and 55 riddles, with audio recordings also uploaded. These may represent separate reporting snapshots or related components and should not automatically be added together.
Wikidata lexemes and language data
A training on improving Igbo lexemes using Broomstick was held online on 29 August 2026; the report lists 48 registrations and 35 attendees. Participants practiced forms, senses, grammatical features, and incomplete lexeme records. A session on 23 May reported 32 registrations and 27 attendees. Duplicity training reported 58 registrations, 50 attendees, and more than 50 Wikipedia-to-Wikidata linkages created or improved.
A dictionary entry gives information about a word; a Wikidata lexeme represents structured linguistic information such as forms and senses. A sitelink connects a page on one Wikimedia project to the corresponding entity on another. Localization helps make software and interfaces usable in a particular language.

2024 financial reporting discrepancy
The first-year grant report records total expenditure of ₦33,471,071.10 for 2024. However, displayed category figures of ₦28,800 for personnel, ₦1,900 for operations, and ₦50,300 for programmatic costs do not reconcile with the reported total. This is a discrepancy in the published online record. It should be checked against the original uploaded financial report or a corrected statement before the category figures are used for financial analysis.
The report also states that the group had no other revenue sources for the reporting period. This should be understood as a statement in that report for that reporting period, not necessarily a permanent condition.
Internal micro-grants
The group's published internal-grant framework states a maximum grant of US$500 or its equivalent in naira. Eligible projects can include expanding Igbo Wikipedia and Wiktionary, improving content quality, promoting awareness of Wikimedia projects, and supporting Wikidata, Commons, Wikiquote, Wikisource, and related projects.
The framework states that applicants should be active in the community online or offline, and proposals or reports may be submitted in English or Igbo. The application directory includes reporting links for the first and second halves of 2024, both halves of 2025, and the first and second quarters of 2026. This indicates a recurring small-grant mechanism but does not prove that every application was funded.

12. Community Development and Geographic Reach
The public record suggests a model built around university fan clubs, local hubs, in-person outreach, virtual training, edit-a-thons, and language documentation. Campus-based groups help students learn editing and participate in campaigns; local meetups can recruit contributors unfamiliar with Wikimedia; online training makes participation possible across locations; and interviews/audio projects capture spoken language and expressions.
The official profile lists an Abuja address associated with Cavic Nigeria, No. 30 Agadez Street, opposite Glo Office, off Aminu Kano Crescent, Wuse 2. This is a profile listing, not verified evidence of current occupancy; confirm before visiting. The profile publishes ndewo@wikimediaigbo.org and ndewoigbowiki@gmail.com as contact addresses in the public record; verify the exact addresses on the live profile before use.
The public record shows that the group's activity is not limited to southeastern Nigeria: membership is described as open to Igbo-literate contributors regardless of location, and the group uses virtual programs alongside local activities.

15. Conclusion
The public evidence describes an established community focused on increasing the representation of Igbo language and culture in digital knowledge. Its development can be traced from the 2016 women-focused Wikipedia project through formal recognition in 2018, expansion into structured data and Wikisource, multi-year grant-supported programming, and the 2026 focus on language data, audio documentation, article quality, and community training.
The most useful starting points for serious research are the official profile, annual reports, multi-year grant record, year-by-year activities, internal-grant reports, and draft by-laws. Together, these provide a foundation for examining the group's history, programs, finances, governance, partnerships, and reported impact.
This dossier is a comprehensive first-pass compilation, not a claim that every publicly available record has been exhausted. Social-media posts, every historical event page, individual contributor histories, external partner publications, and all underlying financial attachments have not been individually audited. A truly exhaustive investigation would require a systematic source-by-source review and direct verification of claims against the underlying records.

Appendix B. Notes on Interpretation and Verification
•	Reported results are attributed to the group's published records unless independently verified.
•	Counts of pages, articles, edits, uploads, lexemes, and participants measure different things and should not be combined without a clear methodology.
•	Registration counts are not the same as attendance, and attendance is not the same as sustained contribution.
•	Grant recommendations, approved budgets, payments, and actual expenditures are distinct financial concepts.
•	Historical staff or team listings should not be represented as the current leadership without a recent source.
•	A draft governance document should not be represented as adopted unless the group publishes an adoption decision or final version.
•	Financial inconsistencies should be reported as discrepancies in the public record, not interpreted as proof of wrongdoing without further evidence.
•	Web pages can change. For formal academic, legal, or financial use, save dated copies or archival snapshots and record access dates.
